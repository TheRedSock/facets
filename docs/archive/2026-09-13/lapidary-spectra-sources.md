# Lapidary spectra & optical-constant sources

Citations and derivations for every optical constant in `data/lapidary/species/`
and `data/lapidary/chromophores/`. The `.tres` files are generated by
`tools/generate_lapidary_data.gd` (kept, labeled); this document is the audit
trail. Verified against `tests/lapidary/test_species_data.gd` (n_D ±0.012,
B–G dispersion ±0.005 vs the published values below).

## Sellmeier convention

`GemSpecies` stores n² − 1 = Σ Bᵢ·λ²/(λ² − Cᵢ) with λ in **micrometers** and
Cᵢ in **µm²**.

- Published tables written as (λ² − Cᵢ²) — Malitson/Dodge corundum, Peter
  diamond — have their C values **squared** before storage.
- Ghosh's quartz equation is already in µm² form (no squaring), and its
  additive constant (0.28604141) is stored as a B term with C = 0
  (λ²/(λ² − 0) ≡ 1), which `GemSpecies.ior_at()` evaluates exactly.
- Known old-repo errors, not carried over: `data/minerals/quartz.tres` was
  **fused silica** (n_D ≈ 1.458, the Wikipedia fused-silica row verbatim);
  `data/minerals/diamond.tres` stored Peter's poles **unsquared**
  (0.0106/0.0175 instead of 0.011236/0.030625); the old topaz (n_D ≈ 1.549)
  and olivine (n_D ≈ 1.605) fits sit far below gem values.

the **gemological** dispersion the trade tables quote.

### Fit method (olivine, topaz, beryl)

No standard published visible-range Sellmeier exists for gem peridot, topaz, or
beryl. The generator fits a 2-term form — one UV pole at 0.025 µm²
(λ₀ ≈ 158 nm, a plausible effective silicate resonance) plus a constant term
(C = 0) — solving B₁ by bisection so n(430.8) − n(686.7) equals the published
gemological dispersion exactly, then B₂ from n_D. Both B terms stay positive
(required by the `ior_at()` guards). Targets:

- **olivine**: n_D = 1.654 = n_α of gem peridot Fo88–92 (RI range 1.654–1.690
  [4][5]); Δn 0.036 spans to n_γ ≈ 1.690. Dispersion 0.020 [5].
- **topaz**: n_D = 1.612, F-rich topaz (1.61–1.62; full species range
  1.606–1.644) [6]; Δn 0.010 (lit. 0.008–0.010); dispersion 0.014 [6]. Note:
  OH-rich Ouro Preto imperial topaz actually measures ~1.629–1.637; we keep the
  species at the common F-type index.
- **beryl**: n_D = 1.577 = typical natural-emerald n_ω (1.575–1.600) [7];
  Δn 0.006 (lit. 0.004–0.010); dispersion 0.014 [7].

## Inclusion vocabularies

Species-typical forms (sub-resources inside each species `.tres`); sizes for
4.5–5.5 mm stones (needles 0.3–1.2 mm, clouds 0.3–0.8 mm):

- **corundum**: rutile silk — NEEDLE sets locked at 60° in the girdle plane
  ((1,0,0), (0.5,0.866,0), (−0.5,0.866,0)), aspect 25, fine + rare CRYSTAL.
- **beryl**: jardin — VEIL sheets + FINGERPRINT rings + two-phase droplet CLOUD.
- **quartz**: milky CLOUD banks + VEILs.
- **olivine**: lily pads — PLATELET (basal-aligned) + matching halo CLOUD
  (two archetypes; the compiler places archetypes independently, so pad+halo
  are statistically paired, not bound).
- **topaz**: tear feathers (VEIL) + CLOUD.
- **diamond**: tiny pinpoint CRYSTAL (weight 1; T8's clarity 1.0 means the
  compiler emits none — rarity comes from grade).

## Fluorescence & zoning

- **corundum**: Cr³⁺ R-line emission ~693 nm, strength 0.5 [8]. Carried by the
  species (the frozen `GemChromophore` has no fluorescence fields), so blue
  sapphire inherits ruby's glow — physically wrong (Fe quenches it); see
  limitations.
- **diamond**: faint blue ~440 nm (N3 centers), strength 0.05.
- **quartz** species carries amethyst's famous sector zoning approximated as
  planar banding (frequency 3, contrast 0.35) since `GemSpecies` zoning is a
  planar field spec; it only shows when `grade.crystal < 1` per the compiler.
- **corundum**: hexagonal growth banding, frequency 6, contrast 0.2.

## Known limitations of the frozen resource classes

1. **Fluorescence lives on `GemSpecies`**, but ruby's 693 nm glow is caused by
   the Cr³⁺ *chromophore* and is iron-quenched in blue sapphire. As authored,
   sapphire inherits corundum's R-line emission. The kernel should gate
   fluorescence by chromophore (or the field should move) in a later pass.
2. **Sector zoning** (amethyst) is angular around the c-axis; `GemSpecies`
   zoning is planar banding (axis/frequency/contrast). Approximated as modest
   planar banding on the quartz species — which also (mildly, and only at
   `crystal < 1`) affects T1 milky quartz, where real milky quartz banding is
   at least plausible.
3. **Lily-pad halo pairing**: the compiler samples archetypes independently, so
   olivine's platelet + halo are two co-tuned archetypes rather than a bound
   pair at one location.
4. **`GemStone.size_mm`** is commented "physical girdle radius", but the
   authored 4.5–5.5 values follow the tier brief's stone scale and the
   Beer–Lambert audit uses 2×size_mm as the reference path; if the kernel
   treats it strictly as a radius, absorption doubles — revisit the comment or
   the values with the kernel contract.
5. **Pleochroism** is authored only where visible at game scale: ruby o/e.
   Peridot's (weak yellow-green/green) and sapphire's are omitted (empty e-ray).
